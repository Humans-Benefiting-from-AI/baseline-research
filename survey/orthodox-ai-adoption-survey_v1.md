# The 2026 Orthodox AI Adoption & Attitudes Survey
*Methodology & Question Bank (v1)*

## Objective
The goal is to produce the first longitudinal, empirically rigorous dataset on how the Orthodox Jewish community (institutions and individuals) uses and views AI. 
This is the asset that builds **empirical authority**. It is highly fundable by foundations and highly citable by journalists.

## Methodology
- **Anonymity:** Fully anonymous to encourage honest reporting on unsanctioned tool usage (especially in Haredi/Yeshivish segments).
- **Segments:** Branching logic for three groups: 
  1. Institutional Leaders (Heads of School, Shul Rabbis, Executive Directors)
  2. Educators & Rebbeim
  3. Laypeople / Consumers
- **Distribution:** Grassroots via WhatsApp groups, Jewish tech newsletters, and LinkedIn. (Avoid official institutional endorsement to maintain independence).

## The Question Bank

### Section 1: Demographics & Segment Routing (All Respondents)
1. Which best describes your primary affiliation? (Modern Orthodox, Yeshivish, Chassidish, Chabad, Sephardic/Edot HaMizrach, Other)
2. What is your primary role in the community? 
   - [ ] Institutional Leadership (Rabbi, Head of School, Board Member) -> *Routes to Section 2*
   - [ ] Educator / Teacher -> *Routes to Section 3*
   - [ ] General Layperson / Professional -> *Routes to Section 4*

### Section 2: Institutional Leaders
3. Has your institution formally discussed a policy regarding AI usage? (Yes, published; Yes, internal only; No, but planned; No)
4. Which of the following operations currently utilize generative AI? (Select all that apply: Grant writing, Communications/Newsletters, Curriculum design, Halachic research, None)
5. What is your primary concern regarding AI in your institution? 
   - Data privacy / leakage of sensitive pastoral data
   - Theological inaccuracy / hallucination of sources
   - Plagiarism by students/staff
   - Erosion of traditional study methods
6. Have you purchased or subscribed to any Jewish-specific AI tools (e.g., Sefaria-integrated bots) for institutional use? (Yes / No)

### Section 3: Educators & Rebbeim
7. How frequently do you use AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, specialized Jewish AI) to prepare shiurim or lesson plans? (Daily, Weekly, Rarely, Never)
8. Do you allow your students to use AI tools for assignments? (Explicitly permitted, Allowed with citation, Explicitly banned, No policy)
9. Have you caught students using AI to generate divrei Torah or essays? (Yes frequently, Yes rarely, No)
10. In your experience, do AI tools accurately reflect the hashkafa (theological worldview) you try to impart to your students? (Usually, Sometimes, Rarely, Never - they are heavily biased)

### Section 4: Consumers & Laypeople
11. Have you ever used an AI tool to ask a Halachic question (a shailah)? (Yes, No)
12. *If Yes to 11:* Did you act on the AI's response without consulting a human Rabbi? (Yes, No)
13. Which platform do you trust most for Jewish text retrieval or explanation? (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Specialized Jewish AI apps, None)
14. How concerned are you about the privacy of your religious queries (e.g., questions about taharat hamishpacha, kashrut, or emunah)? (Very concerned, Somewhat, Not concerned)

### Section 5: The "Un-See-It" Metric (All Respondents)
15. Agree or Disagree: "I believe that within 5 years, AI will fundamentally change how the average Orthodox Jew studies Torah."
16. (Open Text) Describe one specific instance where an AI tool completely failed you or produced an offensive/hilarious Jewish hallucination. (These qualitative quotes make the final report highly readable).
